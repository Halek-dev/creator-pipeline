Getting better output from motion swap

Most bad motion swaps come down to three things: the tool is built for a different job, the inputs are weak, or the clip is rendered at a preview tier. This page explains which tools fit which job, a checklist for inputs, and a one-day test so the team can pick a tool on our own footage.

00:01 · Pick the job first

"Swap" covers two different jobs

Tools sold as "character swap" or "motion swap" do one of two things. Using a tool built for the other job is the most common reason output looks wrong.

Replacement

Keep our clip, change who is in it

  • You get your own footage back
  • Background, camera move and timing stay
  • Only the subject changes
Luma Modify · Runway Aleph · Mage · Pika Pikaswaps · Wan Animate (replace mode)
Motion transfer

Make a new character perform a move

  • Input: a character image plus a driving video
  • Output: a newly generated clip
  • Your original background is not kept
Kling Motion Control 3.0 · Runway Act-Two · Higgsfield Genjutsu · Wan Animate (move mode) · Viggle
00:02 · Diagnose

What the bad output usually means

  • Face changes mid-clip

    Identity drift. Older models drift more; Kling says version 3.0 holds faces better than 2.6. A cleaner reference photo with the face fully visible helps on every tool.

  • Wrong person swapped

    Multiple people in the driving clip. Kling handles one main subject and can pick the wrong face. Crop to one person or use a single-subject clip.

  • Melting hands, sliding feet

    Fast or occluded motion. Simplify the driving video, avoid hands crossing the face, and check that the tool has grounded-physics improvements (Kling 3.0 claims planted feet).

  • Background warps

    Usually a motion-transfer tool being used for a replacement job, or a replacement strength set too high. On Luma, lower Modify Strength to keep the original scene.

  • Soft, blurry result

    Preview tier. Many tools default to 480p or 720p. Test at low resolution, then render finals at 1080p where the plan allows it.

  • Camera drifts or reframes

    Framing mode. Kling's "Match video" copies the reference camera framing; "Match image" keeps the character's pose from the photo. Choose deliberately.

00:05 · Decide on our footage

One-day bake-off

  1. Pick three real clips we've had trouble with. One dance or full-body move, one talking close-up, one with a moving camera.

  2. Choose three tools plus our current one as the baseline. A good starting set: Kling Motion Control 3.0 for body moves, Runway Act-Two for faces, and Luma Modify if we need our own footage back.

  3. Use the same reference photo and clip in every tool. Run each through the input checklist first.

  4. Score each result on the five criteria below, at the resolution we'd actually publish.

  5. Add cost per usable clip, counting retries, and check commercial-use rights on the plan we'd buy.
