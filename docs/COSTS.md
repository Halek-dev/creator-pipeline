# Rent the A40, ration the Seedance credits

The cheapest way to run the project's motion swap is a RunPod A40 pod running Wan Animate 2: about **$0.08 for the 3.4 s Standard clip** measured in Phase 1 and about **$0.23 for a 10 s clip**, rising to $0.12 and $0.37 at High tier; the L40S the docs default to costs about 40% more per clip for 30% more speed, and the A100 used in Phase 2 costs 2.2x the A40 per clip and is never the cheapest choice. Seedance 2.5 through Comfy credits costs **$3.95 for a 10 s 720p swap and $9.73 at 1080p**, 17 to 40 times the A40, because ComfyUI bills ByteDance's token formula at a fixed 1.43x markup and a video-driven swap bills input seconds plus output seconds together; that is exactly why the 14 s 1080p test cost 2,800 credits ($13.27), and it also means the app's 600-credit cap now blocks every 1080p edit, since a 4 s one needs about 820 credits. Idle time, not rendering, drives the pod bill: the 20-minute watchdog allowance, pod boot, model load and review gaps make a realistic month about 1.4 to 1.7x the pure render cost, plus $7 for the 100 GB network volume, so a team rendering 100 ten-second Standard clips a month pays roughly **$40 on an A40, $54 on an L40S, $84 on an A100, $64 on a (not yet built) serverless L40S endpoint, and $395 on Seedance at 720p via Comfy or $276 direct from BytePlus**. Hosted Wan2.2-Animate at $0.04 per output second (WaveSpeed, fal) sets a floor of $40 for those same 100 clips, so a pod beats hosting on price only above roughly 60% utilisation on an A40 or 80% on an L40S; the pod's real advantage is control (seeds, scene presets, the Finish pass, the newer Wan-Animate-2 model), not price. Serverless beats a pod only when a session would hold fewer than about three clips. SCAIL-2 has never been timed in the project; the one published run (571 s per 81 frames on an RTX 3090) implies roughly 1.0 to 1.3x the Wan Animate 2 Standard cost on the same GPU, to be confirmed in the pending Phase 3 session. Every figure below is tagged [M] measured in the project, [P] published by a vendor or benchmark, or [I] inferred by arithmetic from those.

## Per clip, the A40 pod is cheapest and the A100 never is

The project owns three measured anchors for Wan Animate 2. Phase 1 rendered three 81-frame Standard clips (480x848, distilled model, 10 steps) on a RunPod A40 in **464 to 469 s each, about $0.08 per clip** at the pod's roughly $0.60 per hour, with the photoreal test scoring 23/25 ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md), [MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md)). Phase 2 moved to an A100 80 GB PCIe at $1.79 per hour and logged **a Standard pass at about 6 minutes, a High pass (720x1280, full model plus distill LoRA, 6 steps) at about 560 s and $0.28 per seed**, and a Finish pass (SeedVR2 upscale plus FILM interpolation, doubling resolution and frame rate) at about 6 minutes per pass at Standard resolution or 8 at High ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md)). The single app job on an RTX PRO 4500 took 400 s and 318 s for two Standard seeds ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md)). Everything else is inferred: the L40S has never been timed in the project, the RTX 4090 has never been used, SCAIL-2 has never been rendered (the local SCAIL-2 job record is a demo with a 540 s placeholder, not a measurement), and no published benchmark covers any Animate model on an L40S, A40, A100 or H100.

## Idle minutes, not render minutes, set the pod bill

A per-clip figure is only half the bill, because a pod charges from boot to the moment the watchdog stops it. The project's one fully accounted session shows the shape: the Phase 2 A100 session ran about 3.5 hours and cost about $6.30 ([memory, creator-pipeline-goal.md](C:/Users/user/.claude/projects/C--Users-user-Desktop-creator-pipeline/memory/creator-pipeline-goal.md)), while the logged render time sums to roughly 7,400 s, or 2.05 hours and $3.67 ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md)), so **about 41% of that bill was setup, 23 GB of model downloads, an app restart, review time and watchdog idle** [I]. The roadmap's "~$3" for the same session is the render-only share ([ROADMAP.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/ROADMAP.md)). Per finished deliverable, the session's "post it" clip cost $1.02 of render time but about $3.15 if the whole pod bill is divided across the two High-plus-Finish seeds it produced.

The fixed and semi-fixed items are these. The **100 GB network volume costs $7.00 per month** at RunPod's $0.07 per GB-month under 1 TB, billed hourly whether or not a pod is attached ([RunPod serverless pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/pricing), [RunPod pods pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/pricing)); the three engines' models total about 60 to 65 GB on it (wan-animate-2 about 24 GB, wan-animate-2-full +16.5 GB, finishing about 4 GB, scail2 about 20 GB) ([models.json](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/models.json), [ROADMAP.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/ROADMAP.md)), and volumes can grow but never shrink ([RunPod docs](https://docs.runpod.io/storage/network-volumes)). The alternative of leaving a pod stopped is worse: a stopped pod's volume disk bills at $0.20 per GB-month, double the running rate, so 100 GB would cost $20 a month, while the network volume costs $7 and survives termination ([RunPod pods pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/pricing), [RunPod manage pods](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/manage-pods)); terminate-and-redeploy on the network volume is the correct pattern, and the project's pod.py already does it.

Per session, the watchdog's defaults define the idle allowance: **20 minutes after the last job, a 45-minute grace before the first job, checked every 30 s, with stop as the action** ([idle_watchdog.py](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/idle_watchdog.py)). The app's Costs page mirrors this by adding a 20-minute `IDLE_ALLOWANCE_MIN` and a 5-minute `SESSION_OVERHEAD_MIN` to each day's span between first start and last finish ([jobs.py](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/jobs.py)). That means the floor for any session is about 25 minutes of non-render time: on the A40 **$0.25, on the L40S $0.45, on the A100 $0.75** [I], and a session where someone starts the pod and does not queue a job for 45 minutes burns $0.82 on the L40S before a frame renders. The Costs page's pod estimate is a lower bound: it ignores the 45-minute grace, counts only 5 minutes of boot and load, and keys sessions by calendar day ([jobs.py](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/jobs.py)). It is also only as good as `GPU_HOURLY_USD`, which defaults to $1.00 and must be set to the pod's real price at start; the same Phase 1 job appears as $0.14 in the test log and $0.20 in the roadmap because one used the real hourly price and the other the default ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md), [ROADMAP.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/ROADMAP.md)). The watchdog's purpose is to cap the worst case, a forgotten pod, which the runbook prices at **$14 to $24 per day** for the A40 to L40S range ([MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md)) and which would be about $43 per day on the A100 [I].

Two smaller RunPod rules affect cash flow rather than cost. A pod needs at least one hour of credit on the account to deploy, credits are non-refundable and cannot be withdrawn, new accounts can start at $10, and data transfer in and out is free ([RunPod billing](https://docs.runpod.io/get-started/billing-information), [RunPod pods pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/pricing)). Community Cloud would cut the A40 to $0.35 per hour and the L40S to $0.79 ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)), but only by giving up the network volume and re-downloading about 60 GB each session, which the runbook times at about 15 minutes for a first pod ([MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md)); at 100 clips a month that trade saves roughly $14 and adds about 2.5 hours of waiting plus interruption risk, so it is not worth it below several hundred clips a month.

Serverless inverts these fixed costs. Idle is free beyond the 5 s timeout, boot is free (image pulls and cached-model downloads happen before billing starts), and only model load into GPU memory, execution and the idle timeout are billed, rounded up to the second ([RunPod serverless pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/pricing)). The volume still costs $7 a month and pins the workers to its datacentre ([RunPod docs](https://docs.runpod.io/storage/network-volumes)). The Flex hourly rate is 1.5 to 2.4x the equivalent Secure pod (L40S $1.75 vs $1.09, A100 $2.72 vs $1.59, 4090 $1.10 vs $0.74) ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)), and the discounted Active-worker rates are available only through sales ([RunPod serverless pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/pricing)). The break-even against an L40S pod is a session-size question, worked in the final section: below about three clips per session, serverless is cheaper; above it, the pod is.

## Seedance bills input plus output seconds at a 1.43x markup

Seedance 2.5 has no GPU, no volume and no idle: its entire cost is ByteDance's token bill, resold by ComfyUI in credits. The official formula is **tokens = (input video seconds + output video seconds) x output width x output height x output frame rate / 1024**, with input duration zero when the inputs are only text, images or audio, and the API's actual `completion_tokens` as the authoritative figure ([Volcano Engine ModelArk pricing](https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/1544106), [Comfy Partner Node pricing](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/pricing)). At 24 fps that is 48,600 tokens per second at 1080p, 21,600 at 720p and 9,600 at 480p [I from P]. ByteDance's international list (BytePlus ModelArk) prices Seedance 2.5 at **$10.70 per million tokens at 480p and 720p and $11.70 at 1080p with no video input, dropping to $6.40 and $7.00 when a video is attached** ([laozhang.ai](https://blog.laozhang.ai/en/posts/seedance-2-pricing-free-vs-paid-guide.md), [CometAPI](https://www.cometapi.com/seedance-2-5-api-pricing/)); those figures match the Chinese Volcano list of ¥70, ¥77, ¥42 and ¥46 at about ¥6.55 per dollar ([Volcano Engine](https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/1544106)). The official page itself is JS-rendered and was not read directly; the three secondary sources agree with each other and with the CNY list, so the USD figures are treated as published.

ComfyUI converts at a fixed **211 credits per dollar, $0.004739 per credit, at any top-up size** ([Comfy Router pricing](https://docs.comfy.org/development/comfy-router/pricing)), which the project's own $5 purchase confirmed at exactly 1,055 credits ([presets.yaml](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/presets.yaml)). Its Seedance 2.5 table charges **3.229 credits per 1,000 tokens at 480p/720p and 3.530 at 1080p without video input, 1.931 and 2.112 with video input** ([Comfy Partner Node pricing](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/pricing)). Dividing by 211 gives $15.30, $16.73, $9.15 and $10.01 per million tokens, each exactly **1.43x the BytePlus list price**, so Comfy's convenience costs 43% on every Seedance run [I from P]; a source-code reading of the Seedance 2.0 nodes reports the same flat 1.43 multiplier ([InstaSD](https://www.instasd.com/post/seedance-2-0-comfyui-nodes-cost-limits)).

That formula explains the 2,800-credit test. The job record shows a 418-frame, 30 fps source (13.9 s) swapped at 1080p with a reference video attached ([job.json](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/output/_jobs/20261014-223357-a96e/job.json), [TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md)). Because a video was attached, both the input and the output seconds count: (13.9 + 13.9) x 48,600 = about 1.35 million tokens, times 2.112 credits per thousand, is about **2,850 credits; the 2,800 billed is within 2% of that**, the gap being the API's actual token count. A plain 14 s 1080p generation with no video would have been 680,400 tokens x 3.530 = 2,402 credits ($11.38); the same clip costs $9.28 at BytePlus list and cost $13.27 on Comfy. So the "200 credits per second" the app now uses is really 2 x 102.6 credits per billed second, and it is correct only when the reference or source video is the same length as the output. In reference mode with a long driving clip and a short duration setting the ratio is far worse: a 14 s reference driving a 5 s output bills 19 seconds, about 1,950 credits or $9.24 for 5 s of video, $1.85 per output second [I from P]. The project's switch to edit mode as the default (where the output keeps the source clip's length and the duration widget is ignored ([Comfy ByteDance2ReferenceNode](https://docs.comfy.org/built-in-nodes/ByteDance2ReferenceNode))) is therefore the cost-correct choice, and trimming the driving clip to exactly the wanted length is the single biggest Seedance saving available.

The app's current 40/90/200 are within 8% of the same-length edit values, so the pixel-count scaling for 720p and 480p was close; the real fixes are structural. First, the estimate should implement the formula with the input length rather than a flat per-second rate, because a 30 s source swapped at 1080p is 60 billed seconds and about 6,150 credits ($29), and Comfy will let it run into a negative balance that then blocks every Partner Node until a top-up restores it ([Comfy Partner Nodes FAQ](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/faq)). Third, the **600-credit cap in presets.yaml now refuses every 1080p edit**: a 4 s one costs about 820 credits, a 10 s one about 2,050; the cap allows up to 10 s at 480p (371 credits) and 7 s at 720p (583). Either raise it to about 850 to admit the shortest 1080p edit, or accept that High tier on Seedance is unavailable in the app. Fourth, the Fast tier's use of the Seedance 2.5 Draft model needs checking against the edit path: Comfy documents Draft only on the text-, first-last-frame and reference-to-video nodes, drafts render at 480p and are billed as a normal 480p render, the final is a full 1080p render, and drafts expire after 7 days ([Comfy Draft mode](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/bytedance/seedance-2-5-draft), [comfyui-wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-09-25-seedance-2-5-draft-mode)), so draft plus final costs about 18% more than going straight to 1080p and only pays off if it avoids roughly one full re-render in six.

Audio changes nothing on any 2.x route, so turning it off saves nothing. Going direct to BytePlus saves 30% against Comfy; the trade is an account, an API key and an integration outside the ComfyUI graph the app already drives. Comfy's subscriptions do not change the arithmetic: the $20 Standard plan's 4,200 monthly credits are the same 210 per dollar as a top-up, expire monthly and cover about five 10 s 720p edits; only the annual plans reach 262 credits per dollar, and plan credits are spent before top-ups ([comfy.org pricing](https://comfy.org/pricing)). Top-ups expire one year after purchase and are non-refundable ([Comfy Partner Nodes FAQ](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/faq)).

## Hosted Wan Animate at four cents a second is the floor a pod must beat

Three vendors sell Wan Animate per output second, and all three serve **Wan2.2-Animate-14B, the September 2025 model, not the Wan-Animate-2 of August 2026** that the project runs and scored; no hosted Wan-Animate-2 endpoint was found. fal.ai's `wan/v2.2-14b/animate/replace` charges **$0.04 per video second at 480p, $0.06 at 580p and $0.08 at 720p**, counting frames at 16 per billed second, so a 24 fps source bills 1.5 seconds per real second ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan/v2.2-14b/animate/replace)). WaveSpeed's `wan-2.2/animate` charges the same $0.04 and $0.08 per second but on input duration rounded up to whole seconds, with a 3-second minimum and a 120-second cap, and quotes about 92 s per request ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/models/wavespeed-ai/wan-2.2/animate)). Alibaba's own Model Studio charges **$0.18 (std) or $0.26 (pro) per second for animate-mix, the character-replacement mode, in Singapore, and half that in Beijing**; animate-move is $0.12 or $0.18 ([Alibaba Cloud animate-mix](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/wan2-2-animate-mix), [Alibaba Cloud animate-move](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/wan2-2-animate-move)). Replicate publishes no price on its model pages ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/wan-video/wan-2.2-animate-replace)).

|---|---|---|---|

Compared with the pod table, hosting is 2 to 4x the render-only cost of an A40 clip, which sounds like an easy win for the pod until utilisation is counted. The pod's render-only cost per output second at Standard tier is **$0.023 on the A40 (1,395 s per 10 s at $0.60), $0.033 on the L40S, $0.054 on the A100 and about $0.019 on a 4090 at $0.89** [I]; dividing by WaveSpeed's $0.04 gives the fraction of billed pod time that must be spent rendering for the pod to be cheaper: **58% on the A40, 82% on the L40S, 46% on a 4090 (16% if the published 108 s figure holds in the project's graph), and never on the A100**, whose $0.054 already exceeds the hosted rate. At High tier against the $0.08 720p rate the break-evens are 46% (A40), 64% (L40S) and 104% (A100). The Phase 2 session ran at about 59% render utilisation, so an A40 would have roughly tied hosting and the L40S would have lost. A pod that renders a batch of ten or more clips back to back with the watchdog set tight clears the A40 threshold comfortably; a pod started for one or two clips does not.

The honest framing is that the pod is not bought for price. The hosted endpoints run an older model with a fixed pipeline, no seed control, no scene presets, no 2-pass chaining, no Finish pass to 1440x2560 at 48 fps, a 120 s cap, and quality the project has not scored; the 23/25 and 23 to 24/25 results were on Wan Animate 2 ([TEST_LOG.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md)). They are, however, the right fallback for a single urgent 480p clip on a day when nobody wants to start a pod, at $0.40 for 10 s with no session overhead.

Serverless on RunPod is the other way to pay only for use, and its break-even is about session size rather than utilisation. An L40S pod clip costs $0.33 of render plus about $0.10 of in-session gaps (30%, see the next section), with about $0.45 of fixed boot, load and idle per session; a serverless L40S clip costs $0.57 with no session cost. Setting 0.425n + 0.45 = 0.57n gives **n of about 3: sessions of three or fewer 10 s clips are cheaper serverless, larger sessions are cheaper on the pod** [I]. The same arithmetic against an A40 pod ($0.30 per clip variable, $0.25 per session) with a serverless RTX PRO 4500 at about $0.375 per clip gives a break-even of about 3.4 clips, and that serverless card beats the L40S pod outright at any session size, which is the strongest case for eventually building the worker if the team's usage turns out to be scattered single clips rather than batches. The roadmap's judgement that serverless is "worth it only when the team renders a few clips a day on random days" ([ROADMAP.md](C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/ROADMAP.md)) is confirmed by the numbers.

## A hundred ten-second clips cost $40 on an A40 and $395 on Seedance

The monthly scenarios use one representative clip, a **10 s Standard-tier output, one seed, no Finish**, and three volumes: light at 20 clips a month in 4 sessions of 5, team at 100 clips in 10 sessions of 10, heavy at 400 clips in 20 sessions of 20. For pods, the bill is render time plus 30% of render time for in-session gaps (reviewing a result before queuing the next; the Phase 2 session's 41% non-render share included downloads and a restart, so 30% is the steady-state assumption), plus 25 minutes per session of boot, load and the 20-minute watchdog idle, plus $7 for the volume. Serverless and hosted routes carry no session or gap cost; serverless keeps the $7 volume. Seedance uses the same-length edit rates from the credit table. Three things stand out. The spread between the A40 and L40S at team volume is **$14 a month**, smaller than the roughly $17 to $21 a month that session overhead and gaps add on either card, so session discipline (batch the work, queue jobs back to back, keep `IDLE_MINUTES` at 20) is worth more than the GPU choice, and the L40S's 30% speed advantage is a fair price for a team waiting on results. The A100 is the wrong pod at every volume. And Seedance is a different product tier rather than a competitor: at team volume it costs **ten times the A40 route at 720p and 24 times at 1080p**, so it belongs on the clips where its one-shot 30 s output, native 1080p and generated or preserved audio are the point, not as the default engine; once Seedance spend passes about $100 a month, the 30% saving from BytePlus direct is worth the integration work.

Heavy volume changes the operating model more than the arithmetic. Four hundred 10 s clips is 120 hours of L40S render time a month, about 164 pod-hours with gaps and sessions, or seven hours every working day on one pod; at that point a second pod, RunPod's prepaid 3- or 6-month savings plans (discount percentages unpublished) ([RunPod pods pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/pricing)), or Vast.ai's marketplace L40S floor of $0.47 per hour with no SLA ([Vast.ai](https://vast.ai/pricing/gpu/L40S)) all become worth pricing. ## Conclusion

The research reframes the cost question from "which GPU" to "how the team works". The measured and inferred numbers put every rented-GPU route for Wan Animate 2 and SCAIL-2 inside a narrow band of $0.08 to $0.37 per clip, while the two things that actually move the monthly bill are the idle and gap time around rendering, which adds 40 to 70% on top, and whether the clip goes to Seedance at all, which multiplies it by 10 to 40. The project's existing tooling already targets the first lever (the watchdog, the Costs page, the whole-session quote the user asked for); the second lever needs the app's Seedance estimate to become the real token formula with input length, a 4 s floor and a cap that either admits 1080p at about 850 credits or says plainly that it does not.

Two items should change before the Phase 3 GPU session so that its numbers land in the right places: set the default pod back to the A40 (or justify the L40S on speed, not on the "$0.08 either way" claim, which the benchmarks contradict), and record SCAIL-2 and L40S wall-clock times per pass so that the only remaining [I] tags in this report become [M]. The hosted Wan2.2-Animate rate of $0.04 per second is worth keeping on the Costs page as a reference line: if the team's realised pod cost per output second stays above it for a few months, the pod is being started too often for too little, and that is the signal to build the serverless worker.
