# Pick the PRO 4500 low, the PRO 6000 high

Every GPU workload in the creator-pipeline carries the same 16 GB int8 Wan 2.1-class DiT plus about 8 GB of shared encoders, so the tiers differ almost only in activation memory, and that one fact sorts the fleet into two bands. Below 512p (Wan Animate 2 Fast and Standard at 480x848, SCAIL-2 Fast and Standard at 512x896) a **32 GB RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell** is the right card on both billing models: the project measured it at **359 s per Standard pass, equal to the A100** [M], it costs about **$0.072 per pass on a pod at the $0.72 per hour RunPod charged for it and $0.145 per pass on serverless including a 90 s cold start** [I from M and V], and it was in stock in EU-RO-1 on 17 Oct 2026 when the 48 GB A40 and L40S were not. Above 512p (Wan High at 720x1280, SCAIL-2 High at 704x1280, the 40-step author-grade render, and every Finish pass as the graph is built today) the honest VRAM floor is 48 GB for the distilled High tiers and **80 GB for anything involving SeedVR2 on 161 frames or more**, so in a datacentre with no 48 GB stock the choice is between the **A100 80 GB (measured, $1.79 per hour)** and the **RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB ($2.49 per hour, inferred about 2x the A100 PCIe on resident Wan from RunPod's own benchmark, therefore cheaper per clip and able to hold even a 241-frame Finish at 960x1696)**. For serverless, the endpoint should list three GPU types that span tiers rather than three 48 GB cards: a "standard" endpoint with RTX PRO 4500, RTX 5090 and the A40/A6000 48 GB tier, and a "large" endpoint with RTX PRO 6000, A100 and B200, with active workers at zero so nothing bills while idle. Two code changes make the fleet safe to use: a per-tier `min_vram_gb` guard in the app that reads `vram_total` from ComfyUI's `/system_stats` and refuses a tier the card cannot hold (ComfyUI itself never refuses; it offloads or dies mid-render), and inserting `SeedVR2TemporalChunk` plus `SeedVR2TemporalMerge` into the Finish graph, which bounds SeedVR2 memory per chunk and lets 48 GB and even 24 GB cards run the Finish pass that today needs 80 GB. No peak-VRAM figure has ever been logged in this project, so the next pod session should record `nvidia-smi` peaks and pass times per tier on the PRO 4500 and the PRO 6000; that one session turns most of the inferred cells below into measured ones. Tags: [M] measured in the project, [V] vendor published (RunPod, NVIDIA, ComfyUI source or docs), [C] community anecdote, [I] inferred by arithmetic from those.

## One 16 GB DiT, then activations: the requirements per workload

The resident footprint is the same for every tier because ComfyUI keeps the int8_convrot checkpoint quantised in memory and dequantises only inside the matmul; the 16.65 GB file stages as about 15.9 GB of VRAM [C], umt5 fp8 as 6.4 GB, CLIP vision 1.2 GB and SAM 3.1 under 1.7 GB ([comfylab.dev RTX 3090 SCAIL-2 test](https://comfylab.dev/blog/workflows/scail-2-character-replacement-comfyui-workflow/)), consistent with the loader path in the pinned ComfyUI ([ComfyUI/comfy/utils.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/utils.py) lines 1434-1500, [ops.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/ops.py) lines 1401-1460). What varies is activation memory, which ComfyUI itself plans as `area x dtype_bytes x 0.01 x 0.9 MiB` summed over the input latent and every video-sized conditioning latent ([ComfyUI/comfy/model_base.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/model_base.py) lines 418-440; Wan factor 0.9 in [supported_models.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/supported_models.py) line 1343). That formula gives **about 5 GB for Wan at 480p, 12 GB for Wan at 720p and for SCAIL-2 at 512p, and 23 GB for SCAIL-2 at 704p** [V, ComfyUI source arithmetic], and it doubles when cfg is above 1 because cond and uncond are batched, which is why the 40-step cfg 4 render lands at 23 GB and 45 GB of activations [I]. SCAIL-2 needs about twice the activation memory of Wan Animate 2 at the same resolution because it carries four video-sized latents (input, pose, reference mask, SAM) instead of two ([model_base.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/model_base.py) lines 1966, 2010), so the "24 GB works" headroom the project found for Wan does not transfer to SCAIL.

The one workload whose memory grows with clip length is the Finish pass. `with_finishing` wires `VAEEncodeTiled` into `SeedVR2Conditioning` and then into **a single `KSampler` over the whole latent** with no `SeedVR2TemporalChunk` node ([tools/build_workflows.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/build_workflows.py) lines 98-118; the same function is applied to SCAIL graphs in [build_scail.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/build_scail.py) lines 155-158). Only the VAE and the colour post-process are chunked. ComfyUI's own fitted law for the SeedVR2 sampler is `max_latent_frames = (free_GiB - 8.5 - 4 x 0.55) / (0.55 x megapixels)` ([ComfyUI/comfy/ldm/seedvr/constants.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/ldm/seedvr/constants.py) lines 3-11) [V], which inverted says a 960x1696 Finish needs about **30, 47 and 65 GiB for 81, 161 and 241 frames**, and a 1440x2560 Finish about **53, 94 and 134 GiB** [I from V]. The project rendered 161 frames at 1440x2560 on an 80 GB A100 ([docs/TEST_LOG.md](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md) line 22) [M], which the 94 GiB prediction says should not fit; the law carries a four-sigma margin and an 8.5 GiB reserve and ComfyUI's dynamic allocator absorbs some of the excess, so treat the law as conservative by at least 15 percent, but also treat 241 frames at 1440x2560 (134 GiB) as a job that fails on every card except a B200 until it is either measured or the graph is chunked.

System RAM is not the binding constraint on the pods the project has used. ComfyUI reads the cgroup limit, printed "RAM limited by cgroup to 59128 MB" on the RTX PRO 4500 pod [M, session log], and caps pinned host memory at about 41.7 GiB under that limit ([ComfyUI/comfy/model_management.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/model_management.py) lines 364-369, 1636-1645) [V]. Measured working sets are 12 to 15 GB extra for Wan Animate 2 int8 with the pose cache on the GPU ([nemovideo RTX 4090 review](https://www.nemovideo.com/pt-BR/blog/ai-video-models/wan-animate-2-comfyui-review)) [C] and about 22 GB peak for SCAIL-2 int8 on a 32 GB machine ([comfylab.dev](https://comfylab.dev/blog/workflows/scail-2-character-replacement-comfyui-workflow/)) [C]. The one configuration that approaches the cgroup is Wan High with the pose cache moved to the CPU in bf16: the cache costs about 12.5 GB of system RAM at 480x832 ([ComfyUI Wiki, 7 Aug 2026](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-07-wan-animate-2)) [V] and 720x1280 has 2.26x the tokens, so about 28 GB in bf16 or 14 GB in int8 [I]; keep `dtype int8` on the cache node if it is moved, which the workflow already sets ([workflows/wan_animate2.json](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/workflows/wan_animate2.json) node 2).

## The PRO 4500 and PRO 6000 bracket the useful fleet

Three GPU generations are on offer and they differ in one way that matters for this project's checkpoints. Ampere (A40, A6000, A100) has INT8 tensor cores but no FP8; Ada (4090, L4, L40S) adds FP8; Blackwell (5090, PRO 4500, PRO 6000, B200) adds NVFP4 ([comfy-kitchen README](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/comfy-kitchen): FP8 layout needs SM 8.9, NVFP4 and MXFP8 need SM 10.0, `int8_linear` runs on every backend). The project's int8_convrot DiTs were built for exactly this situation: the format is "optimized for Ampere GPUs" and runs on native INT8 GEMMs everywhere ([Winnougan/Wan2.2-INT8-Convrot](https://huggingface.co/Winnougan/Wan2.2-INT8-Convrot)) [C], so no card in the list is disqualified by precision. The umt5 fp8_scaled text encoder dequantises to bf16 on Ampere, a VRAM saving with no speed-up, which is what RunPod's benchmark shows when the A100 PCIe runs fp8 and fp16 Wan 2.2 in the same 1,456 s ([RunPod, best GPU for AI video, updated 12 Oct 2026](https://www.runpod.io/articles/guides/best-gpu-for-ai-video)) [V]. FP8 on cards that hold the model buys 6 to 11 percent (PRO 6000 734 to 656 s, H100 SXM 652 to 616 s) [V], not 2x, and NVFP4's claimed 2x comes from Comfy's image-model tests on cu130 PyTorch with no Wan measurement anywhere ([ComfyUI blog, 9 Jan 2026](https://blog.comfy.org/p/new-comfyui-optimizations-for-nvidia)) [V]; it should not influence the choice.

Speed for a resident single 14B model is where published numbers are thin and the project's own anchors matter most. RunPod's Wan 2.2 table is an offload benchmark for every card under 80 GB (its sub-80 GB rows show 2.5 to 15 GB peak VRAM because the template streamed two 14B experts from host RAM) and should not be read as the speed of a properly resident model: the project's 359 s PRO 4500 Standard pass is 4.7x faster than RunPod's 1,681 s for the same card ([RunPod benchmark](https://www.runpod.io/articles/guides/best-gpu-for-ai-video); [docs/TEST_LOG.md](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/TEST_LOG.md) line 12) [V, M]. RunPod's resident rows are usable as ratios: the RTX PRO 6000 at 656 s against the A100 PCIe at 1,456 s is **2.2x**, the H100 PCIe 978 s is 1.5x, the B200 507 s is 2.9x [V]. The project's own ratio is A40 465 s to A100 360 s, 1.3x [M], and the L40S is set equal to the A100 because the two were within 1 percent on Wan 2.2 A14B in an independent run ([SaladCloud](https://blog.salad.com/benchmarking-wan2-2/)) [V]. The RTX 5090 has no resident Wan datapoint; in RunPod's fits-in-VRAM LTX-2.3 run the PRO 4500 beat it (13.2 s against 15.3 s) while in the offloaded Wan fp16 run the 5090 was 1.6x faster, so about 300 s per pass, between parity and a third faster than the PRO 4500, is the honest estimate [I]. The L4 is a bad fit at 300 GB/s of bandwidth ([gpuperhour L4](https://gpuperhour.com/compare/l4-vs-rtx-6000-ada)) [V] and the slowest card in RunPod's table at 4,138 s; the 4090's published 108 s is a warm 480x832 run with the cache on a graph that is not the project's, so 250 s is a guess bounded by the A40 ratio [I].

## Verdicts: PRO 4500 for 512p and below, PRO 6000 or A100 above

The verdicts below hold the team's stated order of priorities: no idle charges on serverless (active workers 0, idle timeout at the 5 s default or at most 30 s, FlashBoot left on since paused workers are not billed ([RunPod serverless pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/pricing)) [V]), reliability (every type on an endpoint must hold the workload, because a job that OOMs late bills to the second it died ([RunPod billing blog, Oct 2026](https://www.runpod.io/blog/failed-requests-real-charges-how-runpod-serverless-billing-works-when-workers-fail)) [V]), then cost per clip. RunPod lets an endpoint list up to three GPU types and its scheduler since 26 Aug 2026 scores all of them together, placing a worker on whichever is free, but it never expands beyond the types or datacentres you allowed, and the blog's advice is to "size the model to the VRAM floor of the list" ([RunPod blog, how Serverless places workers](https://www.runpod.io/blog/serverless-places-workers); [RunPod endpoint configurations](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/endpoints/endpoint-configurations)) [V]. Since RunPod offers no per-request GPU choice, the clean way to give small jobs cheap cards and big jobs big cards is **two endpoints on the same image and the same volume**: the volume is meant to "share data across multiple machines and Runpod products" and the project's outputs are already namespaced per job and seed ([RunPod network volumes](https://docs.runpod.io/storage/network-volumes); [app/jobs.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/jobs.py) lines 152-159) [V, M], though attaching one volume to two endpoints should be confirmed in the console before relying on it.

## When EU-RO-1 runs dry: span tiers first, add a volume second, Global Volumes last

The stock-out of 17 Oct 2026 is structural, not an accident. A network volume is bound to its datacentre for life, pins any endpoint that mounts it, and RunPod publishes no per-datacentre GPU list; the only sources are the console picker and the v2 catalog API, `GET /v2/catalog/datacenters?include=GPU_AVAILABILITY`, whose per-GPU `availability` is `NONE`, `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, with the GraphQL `stockStatus` field as the pod-side equivalent ([RunPod API v2, list data centers](https://docs.runpod.io/api-reference-v2/catalog/list-data-centers.md); [RunPod GraphQL API](https://docs.runpod.io/graphql-api)) [V]. The eight cards the console showed span the 24, 32, 80, 96 and 180 GB tiers; both 48 GB pools (`AMPERE_48` for A40 and A6000, `ADA_48_PRO` for L40, L40S and 6000 Ada) were absent, which is the `availability: NONE` state ([RunPod GPU types reference](https://docs.runpod.io/references/gpu-types)) [V]. When every type an endpoint lists is unavailable in the pinned datacentre, jobs sit `IN_QUEUE`, workers show as "throttled" ("The hosts holding your workers have no free GPUs"), and RunPod documents no automatic failure or maximum queue time; the request's own `ttl` (default 24 hours) is what eventually deletes the job ([RunPod serverless troubleshooting](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/troubleshooting); [RunPod send requests](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/endpoints/send-requests)) [V]. A Wan Animate 2 worker author hit exactly this in September 2026 when an automated test failed "because no 48 GB GPU was free" ([PrinceMargaret/wan2.2-animate-2 PR #2](https://github.com/PrinceMargaret/wan2.2-animate-2/pull/2)) [C].

The first and cheapest defence is the one the verdict table already applies: list three GPU types that span tiers, so that a missing tier does not strand the queue, and check inventory before deploying a pod. `tools/pod.py` accepts any `gpuTypeId` ([tools/pod.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/pod.py) line 124), so a `--list-gpus` option that prints the catalog API's availability for EU-RO-1 costs about 20 lines and turns a surprise into a choice; the Runner should also surface a job that has sat `IN_QUEUE` longer than the expected cold start plus a margin, with a one-click endpoint reset, which is the fix practitioners report for stuck queues ([dev.to, RunPod serverless jobs stuck in queue](https://dev.to/ownstackhq/runpod-serverless-jobs-stuck-in-queue-forever-after-changing-modelid-47g5)) [C].

The second defence is a second network volume in another European datacentre. RunPod names EU-CZ-1, EU-FR-1, EU-NL-1, EU-SE-1 and EUR-IS-2 alongside EU-RO-1 ([RunPod blog, Global Networking expansion, updated 13 Sep 2026](https://www.runpod.io/blog/runpod-global-networking-expansion)) [V]; the one to pick is whichever the catalog API shows carrying both 48 GB pools at `HIGH`. Filling it means re-downloading 60 to 65 GB, about 15 minutes on a first pod per the runbook ([docs/MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/docs/MOTION_SWAP_RUNBOOK.md) line 32) [M], or an rsync between two pods, the only migration RunPod documents ([RunPod network volumes](https://docs.runpod.io/storage/network-volumes)) [V], and it costs another $7 per month for 100 GB. For pods this is clean: deploy wherever the card is. For serverless it is half a solution: an endpoint can attach one volume per datacentre and will spread workers across them, but "data does not sync automatically between volumes" [V], so a result rendered in EU-CZ-1 lands on the EU-CZ-1 volume while the app host reads EU-RO-1; the app would have to fetch it over RunPod's S3-compatible API, which both EU-RO-1 and EU-CZ-1 support ([RunPod S3-compatible API](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/storage/s3-api)) [V], roughly the 200 lines of storage code the serverless design deliberately avoided.

The third option is RunPod's own answer to the pin, and it is not ready to carry this project yet. Global Volumes, extended to Serverless on 8 Oct 2026, are region-independent, mount at `/runpod-volume`, and let "workers start in any data center with your GPU types"; but they are object-backed with no file locking or atomic rename, writes are last-write-wins with eventual consistency, billing is per request ($0.005 per 1,000 writes, $0.0005 per 1,000 reads) with the storage price unpublished, an endpoint cannot combine one with a network volume, and RunPod's own warning is that "a full ComfyUI setup or a virtual environment ... will fail or run slowly" on it ([RunPod blog, Global Volumes for Serverless beta](https://www.runpod.io/blog/global-volumes-serverless-beta); [RunPod docs, Global volumes overview](https://docs.runpod.io/storage/globalvolume/overview.md)) [V]. The project's venv lives on the volume today ([scripts/setup.sh](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/scripts/setup.sh) lines 44-59), so a Global Volume would mean moving the install into the worker image and keeping only models and outputs on the volume, which the serverless design already proposes. It is worth a trial for model weights once the beta publishes throughput and a storage price; it is not the plan. Leaving RunPod is a bigger step still: Modal has fixed per-second prices (L40S $0.000542, A100 80 GB $0.000694, RTX PRO 6000 $0.000842) and no stock management, but no 5090, no PRO 4500, no way to mount the existing volume, and a full rewrite ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)) [V].

## Two code changes: a VRAM guard in the app, temporal chunking in Finish

ComfyUI will always try to run. In NORMAL and LOW_VRAM states it budgets weights as `max(free - minimum_required, min(free x 0.4, free - inference_memory))` and streams whatever does not fit from host RAM every step ([ComfyUI/comfy/model_management.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy/model_management.py) lines 877-891, 1020-1034) [V], which is how a 3090 renders SCAIL-2 at 571 s [C] and how a 32 GB card would attempt SCAIL-2 at 704p with about 12 GB of DiT offloaded, moving roughly 70 to 120 GB of weights per segment over PCIe [I]. The penalty is moderate for a 6 to 10 step distilled pass and ruinous for 40 steps, and when the activations alone exceed the card the result is an OOM after the billed minutes are gone. The app has no check at all today: `/system_stats` is read only by the smoke test ([tools/smoke_test.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/smoke_test.py) lines 55-61), `grep system_stats app/` finds nothing, the SCAIL tier table carries size, steps, strength and a time factor but no VRAM field ([app/jobs.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/jobs.py) lines 166-170), and the preset's "needs ... a 32 GB+ GPU" is a comment nobody enforces ([app/presets.yaml](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/app/presets.yaml) line 71).

The guard is small. `/system_stats` returns `devices[0].vram_total` in bytes alongside `vram_free`, `torch_vram_total` and the cgroup-aware `system.ram_total` ([ComfyUI/server.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/server.py) lines 752-803) [V]. Give every tier a `min_vram_gib` (Wan Fast and Standard 22; Wan High 30 with the cache on cpu, 46 with it on gpu; SCAIL Fast and Standard 28; SCAIL High 40; SCAIL 40-step 40 at 512p and 62 at 704p; Finish as built computed per job as `10.7 + 0.55 x megapixels x latent_frames` GiB with latent frames 21, 41 and 61 for 81, 161 and 241 pixel frames, or a flat 20 once chunking is in) [I from V and C], read `vram_total` once at job creation, store the GPU name and GiB on the job so the Costs page and the card can show them, and refuse the tier with a message that names the card, the requirement and the nearest tier that fits. Comparisons should be in GiB (a 48 GB card reports about 51.5e9 bytes, 48 GiB). An explicit "allow with offload" override keeps the 24 GB Wan and 32 GB SCAIL-High experiments possible and marks the job so its time is not mistaken for a resident measurement. On serverless the same check belongs in the handler as a pre-sampling step, returning a clear error before the GPU does any work, because the app cannot know in advance which of the endpoint's three types will pick the job up; the two-endpoint split above is what keeps that error rare.

The Finish fix is the larger win because it moves the heaviest workload from 80 GB to any card. The pinned ComfyUI ships `SeedVR2TemporalChunk`, described as "Split a SeedVR2 video latent into overlapping temporal chunks small enough to sample one at a time within VRAM, wiring latents outputs to both Apply SeedVR2 Conditioning and the sampler latent input before recombining with Merge SeedVR2 Latents", with a `chunking_mode` of `auto` (predict the largest chunk that fits free VRAM) or `manual` (`frames_per_chunk`, a 4n+1 pixel-frame count, default 21) and a `temporal_overlap` in latent frames that is crossfaded at merge ([ComfyUI/comfy_extras/nodes_seedvr.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/ComfyUI/comfy_extras/nodes_seedvr.py) lines 423-500) [V]. In `with_finishing`, insert the chunk node between `Upscale Encode` and the pair `Upscale Cond` / `Upscale Sampler`, feed its latents output to both, and insert `SeedVR2TemporalMerge` between the sampler and `Upscale Decode`, passing the chunk node's `temporal_overlap` output through ([tools/build_workflows.py](file:///C:/Users/user/Desktop/creator-pipeline/tools/build_workflows.py) lines 108-111). With `auto`, the per-chunk budget is free VRAM minus 10.7 GiB, so at 1440x2560 a 24 GB card samples roughly 13 to 17 pixel frames per chunk, a 48 GB card about 61 and an 80 GB card about 121 [I from V]; at 960x1696 the 48 GB card gets over 100 frames per chunk, so a 161-frame Standard-tier Finish becomes two chunks and a 241-frame one three. Total sampler time is roughly unchanged (the same frames are processed) plus the overlap, a few to 20 percent depending on chunk count, and the VAE and colour stages are already chunked. A nonzero overlap of about 4 latent frames (16 pixel frames) is the right default to avoid a visible seam; `tools/validate_workflow.py` and one A100 or PRO 4500 run comparing chunked and unchunked output on the Phase 2 clips are the acceptance test. After this change the Finish rows in the requirements table collapse to "24 GB minimum, 48 GB comfortable", the Standard endpoint can run Standard-tier Finish, and the only workload left that needs 80 GB is the 40-step render at 704p.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's GPU question from "which 48 GB card" to "which of two bands", and the answer in each band is a Blackwell workstation part the earlier reports did not price for pods: the RTX PRO 4500 at the bottom, where it is measured to match the A100 for 40 percent of the hourly rate, and the RTX PRO 6000 at the top, where its 96 GB and inferred 2x make it the first 80 GB-class card that is cheaper per clip than the A100 rather than merely faster. The A40 and L40S, the cards every runbook and default in the repository name, turn out to be neither the cheapest nor the available choice in the project's own datacentre, and their absence on 17 Oct 2026 is a reminder that a network volume buys cheap storage at the price of a single inventory.

The more durable lesson is that VRAM headroom in this pipeline is set by the graph, not the model. The 16 GB DiT fits a 24 GB card; it is the Finish pass's unchunked sampler and the High tiers' conditioning latents that push the requirement to 48 and 80 GB, and one of those is a 20-line builder change. Once `SeedVR2TemporalChunk` is in the graph and the app refuses tiers a card cannot hold, the fleet decision stops being a daily judgement call: Standard-tier work, including its Finish, goes to the cheapest 32 GB card in stock, and only SCAIL-2 at 704p, Wan High with the GPU cache and the 40-step render still need the big card. The next pod session is worth more than any further desk research; it is the first time the project will know its own peaks.
